What a "B+" Financial Grade Actually Means

No black box, no secret sauce — here's the exact weighting behind every grade Grade My Finance produces.

The five factors behind every grade

Your overall score is a weighted blend of five factors:

  • Income strength — 25%, measured against a benchmark that adjusts for your age
  • Savings rate — 20%, how much of your take-home pay you're keeping vs. spending
  • Non-mortgage debt — 20%, weighted by type (more on this below)
  • Age-based net worth — 20%, your net worth compared to a typical multiple of your spending for your age
  • Emergency fund — 15%, months of expenses covered by cash savings

Why income strength carries the most weight

Income is the resource every other factor draws from — it's what funds savings, pays down debt, and builds net worth over time. Two people can have identical habits, but the one with a stronger income has more room to translate those habits into results, which is why it's weighted slightly higher than the others.

Why debt isn't just "debt = bad"

Not all debt behaves the same way, so the debt factor doesn't either. Credit card balances count the most heavily against your score, since they typically carry the highest interest rates and the least favorable terms. Personal loans count moderately-high, auto loans moderately, and student loans lightest — reflecting that student debt is typically lower-rate, more expected at certain life stages, and often comes with more flexible repayment options.

What a B+ specifically signals

A B+ generally means you're doing well across most factors with no major red flags, but at least one area — often the "weakest link" your report card calls out specifically — has clear room to improve. It's rarely an even B+ across the board; more often it's a mix of strong and middling factors that average out to that letter.

Why grades aren't guarantees

A grade is a snapshot built on benchmarks, not a certification of financial health or a promise about your future. It's meant to give you a fast, honest read on where you stand today and a specific, ranked list of what would move the needle — not a verdict on your worth or your decisions.

Frequently asked questions

Can two people with the same net worth get different grades?

Yes. Net worth is compared against an age-adjusted benchmark, not a flat number, and it's only one of five weighted factors — income, savings rate, debt, and emergency fund all factor in independently.

Is a lower grade a bad thing?

Not in the sense of a judgment — think of it more as a snapshot and a to-do list. The report card is specifically designed to show you the 1-3 moves that would raise your grade fastest, ranked by actual point impact.
