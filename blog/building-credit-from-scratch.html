How to Build Credit From Scratch

No credit history isn't the same problem as bad credit — but it can be just as limiting. Here's how to actually start a file.

Why "no history" can be as limiting as "bad credit"

Credit scores are calculated from a track record — payment history, account age, utilization. With nothing to measure yet, many scoring models simply can't generate a reliable score at all, which can mean automatic denials from lenders who require a minimum history length, regardless of your actual financial responsibility.

Common starting points

  • Secured credit cards — you put down a deposit (often $200-500) that typically becomes your credit limit; used and paid on time, it reports to the bureaus just like a regular card
  • Becoming an authorized user — being added to a family member's well-managed existing card can let their positive history reflect on your file too, though this depends entirely on their card's payment behavior
  • Credit-builder loans — a loan structure where you make payments first, and only receive the loan amount (held in a locked account) once it's paid off; the payments themselves build payment history
  • Retail store cards — often easier to qualify for than general cards, though usually carry higher interest rates, so paying in full each month matters even more

The habits that actually matter once you have an account

  • Pay in full and on time, every cycle — this is the single largest factor in most scoring models
  • Keep utilization low — using a small fraction of your available limit, commonly cited under 30%, tends to help more than using most or all of it even if paid off monthly
  • Don't close your first card once you get others — account age matters, and closing your oldest account can shorten your average credit history

A realistic timeline

Most scoring models need at least a few months of reporting history before generating a score at all, and a "good" score generally takes sustained good habits over a longer stretch — often a year or more — rather than a fast fix. There's no legitimate shortcut to a long track record; consistency over time is the actual mechanism.

Frequently asked questions

Do debit cards help build credit?

No — debit cards draw from your own bank balance and generally aren't reported to credit bureaus at all, so they don't contribute to building a credit history.

Is it bad to have no credit history at all?

It's not a mark against you the way negative history is, but it can still limit approval odds for loans and cards that require a minimum credit history, since there's no track record to evaluate.
