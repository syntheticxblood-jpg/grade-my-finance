Should You Buy or Lease a Car?

This comes up constantly, and the honest answer is that neither buying nor leasing is universally better — it depends on how long you keep cars, how much you drive, and what you actually value.

The core structural difference

Buying means paying for the full value of the car (usually financed), building equity as the loan is paid down, and owning an asset at the end. Leasing means paying for the vehicle's depreciation over the lease term, with lower payments but no ownership at the end.

Where leasing tends to make more sense

Someone who likes driving a newer car every few years, doesn't drive unusually high mileage, and prefers predictable, generally lower monthly payments without a large repair bill risk (since leased cars are typically under warranty) may find leasing genuinely fits their preferences.

Where buying tends to make more sense

Someone who drives a lot of miles, prefers to keep a car for many years past when payments end, or wants to build equity in an asset rather than pay indefinitely for temporary use generally comes out ahead financially by buying, especially over a longer time horizon.

The math most people skip

Buying and keeping a car for years after the loan is paid off means several years of no payment at all — a leased car, by contrast, almost always has some ongoing payment, since a new lease typically follows the old one. Comparing total cost over a long time horizon (not just the first few years) usually favors buying.

What actually determines the right answer

Mileage limits and fees for exceeding them, how long you genuinely tend to keep vehicles, and how much predictability versus ownership you personally value all matter more than any generic rule — this is a case where the "right" answer is legitimately personal.

Frequently asked questions

Is it ever cheaper to lease than to buy?

In the short term, often yes — lease payments are frequently lower than loan payments on the same vehicle, since you're only paying for depreciation, not the full value. The comparison shifts over a longer time horizon.

Can you negotiate the price on a lease the same way as a purchase?

Yes — the vehicle's negotiated price affects lease payments too, since it factors into the depreciation calculation. It's a common mistake to skip negotiating on a lease.
