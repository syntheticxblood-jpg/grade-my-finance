    Methodology, assumptions & privacy
    • Weighting: Income Strength (25%), Savings Rate (20%), Non-mortgage Debt (20%), Age-based Net Worth (20%), Emergency Fund (15%).
    • Within the debt factor, credit card balances count most heavily, personal loans/other debt moderately-high, auto loans moderately, and student loans lightest — reflecting typical interest rates and repayment flexibility, not a judgment on any specific loan.
    • Crypto and other investments count as assets alongside retirement, brokerage, and cash.
    • Targets: savings rate ≈ 25% of take-home; emergency fund ≈ 6 months; age-based net worth uses a baseline of your annual spend (or $30k minimum).
    • Income is compared against a typical benchmark for your age (roughly $38k at 22 up to $80k by 55+), not one flat number for everyone.
    • Negative net worth doesn't automatically zero out — it's fairly common at younger ages (student loans, no home equity yet), so this factor gives some credit for "typical for this age" vs. treating any negative number identically.
    • The "how you compare" numbers are rough estimates for context, not exact data.
    • Privacy: your income, expenses, assets, and debts are entered and calculated entirely in your browser and never transmitted anywhere. Your inputs are saved automatically to your own device's local storage (not sent to us) so they're there next time you visit. When you complete a grade, we log only your letter grade and numeric score (no financial details) to power the public "report cards graded" counter. See our Privacy Policy for full details.
    • Currency: the selector converts displayed amounts using live exchange rates, but the underlying benchmarks (median income, net worth targets) are calibrated to the US — your grade doesn't adjust for cost-of-living differences in other countries.

    Assumes you keep investing your current monthly surplus (income minus expenses) at this return, compounded annually. Doesn't account for inflation, market swings, or life changes — an estimate, not a promise.

    Current path With these events
    Each event's dollar effect (down payment, new expense, income change) is applied on top of your current numbers — an illustration of the shape of the impact, not a precise forecast.
    Freedom number explorer ★ PRO —

    The "safe withdrawal rate" is the assumption behind every FI number you've ever seen — drag it and watch your actual target move.

    Your "number" = annual spending ÷ withdrawal rate. A lower rate is more conservative (bigger number, more cushion); a higher rate assumes your money needs to last less time or you're comfortable with more risk.

    FAQ & disclaimer

    Do you store my financial data?

    Your income, expenses, assets, and debts are entered and calculated entirely in your browser — we never receive or see your actual financial figures. Your inputs are automatically saved to your own device's local storage (not sent to us) so they're there next time you open the site. When you complete a grade, we do log your letter grade and numeric score (not your financial details) to power our public counter of report cards graded. Full details in our Privacy Policy.

    Is this financial advice?

    No. Grade My Finance is for educational purposes only and is not a substitute for professional financial advice. Always do your own research or consult a licensed advisor.

    How is my grade calculated?

    We use weighted factors: age-based net worth, income strength, savings rate, emergency fund size, and non-mortgage debt levels. Scores are benchmarks, not guarantees. See the methodology in your report card for exact weights.
